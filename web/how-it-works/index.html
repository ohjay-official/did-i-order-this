How it works

A parcel email, taken apart

A delivery message can look official and still be a lure. The product splits the work on purpose: a model reads the wording, and code decides the verdict.

01

The email arrives

This is the sample DHL hold used in the demo. It cites a tracking number and asks for a $2.99 release fee.

From DHL Express Desk <alerts@dhl-parcel-desk.example>

Subject Your parcel is held. Pay the release fee.

Your parcel could not be delivered and is being held at a DHL facility. Tracking number: GM123456789012345. A customs release fee of $2.99 is due.

02

The model reads the claim

On-device AI, through Firebase AI Logic, turns the wording into a structured claim. It does not issue Safe, Warning, or Neutral.

Claims to be
DHL, as a carrier, at dhl-parcel-desk.example
Asks you to
Pay a release fee
Cites
Tracking GM123456789012345

03

Code looks at what you actually ordered

The comparison uses the orders and tracking numbers stored on this device. Imports are parsed in the browser and are not uploaded. On the default demo set you are not expecting any parcel.

Expected parcels on this device

None

The claimed domain dhl-parcel-desk.example is not in the official-domain pack.

04

A stamp, then the verdict

Matching tracking is marked safe. No match is a warning. The source label always says where the check ran: on this device, in the cloud after you agreed, as a limited check with no AI, or not completed.

warning

You're not expecting any parcel right now.

Checked on this device
