The fine print

Privacy policy

Last updated: 23 November 2026. Applies to did-i-order-this.vercel.app and the sideloaded Chrome extension.

Who runs this

Did I Order This? is a hackathon project built by Oh-Jay for #BuildHybridAI 2026. There is no company behind it, no account system, and nothing to buy.

What is collected

  • Orders, tracking numbers and senders you save. Stored only on your device: in this browser's IndexedDB on the website, or in chrome.storage.local in the extension. They are never uploaded.
  • Files you import (CSV or ZIP order history). Read in the browser and turned into saved orders. The files are not uploaded.
  • Email text you paste or open. Read on your device. The email text leaves the device only in the cloud-email case below.
  • A next-step tip on the hosted site. After a verdict, the site may pick one fixed next-step sentence. In the cloud, and only after you agreed to the cloud for that check (a Check in the cloud yes; the Allow cloud switch alone is not enough), it sends only the verdict outcome, the action type (pay, log in, download, or none) and a brand name when the real sender domain is official. It never sends the email body, tracking or order numbers, or the sender address. Without that agreement it runs on your device or not at all. The extension does not make this call.
  • Usage analytics. Page views and performance numbers, described under Analytics. No email text, no saved orders, no names.

We do not ask for a name, an email address, a phone number or a payment card, and we do not use the Gmail API or scan a mailbox.

On-device and cloud data flow

  1. Every check starts on your device, using Chrome's built-in model through Firebase AI Logic.
  2. If on-device AI is not available, the hosted site asks first. Only if you choose Check in the cloud does the text of that one email go to the cloud. You can choose Do not send or run a limited check with no AI instead.
  3. After a verdict on the hosted site, a separate next-step tip may be picked. It goes to the cloud only after you agreed to the cloud for that check, and it never includes the email body; only then does it load App Check and reCAPTCHA. Otherwise it runs on your device, or not at all.
  4. The Chrome extension never sends email text to the cloud, and it does not request a next-step tip.
  5. The verdict is computed on your device by comparing the model's reading with the orders you saved. A failed next-step call never changes that verdict.

Firebase and Google processing

Cloud reads go through Google's Firebase AI Logic to the Gemini Developer API. Requests are protected by Firebase App Check with reCAPTCHA, which Google loads for a cloud email read you agreed to, and also for the hosted site's next-step tip, to tell real visitors from scripts. An on-device email read does not send the email or load reCAPTCHA. Choosing Do not send skips both the email cloud read and the next-step tip, so nothing is loaded from Google. reCAPTCHA may set its own security cookie and stores a _grecaptcha value in local storage. Google processes these requests under its own terms and the Google Privacy Policy.

The project uses the free quota of the Gemini API. Under Google's Gemini API Additional Terms, Google may use content sent to the unpaid quota, and the responses, to improve its products, and human reviewers may read it after it is disconnected from the project. In the EEA, Switzerland and the UK, Google applies its paid-service terms instead. Do not send an email that holds information you would not share with Google.

Analytics

The website uses Vercel Web Analytics and Vercel Speed Insights. Both are cookie-free: they set no cookies and do not track you across sites. They record the page path (we strip query strings), referrer, browser, operating system, device type, country and page speed numbers. Vercel identifies a visit with a hash that resets daily and does not store your IP address. Because nothing here sets a tracking cookie, there is no cookie banner. The only cookie you may see is reCAPTCHA's security cookie, after a cloud email read you agreed to, or after the hosted site's next-step tip.

The site also keeps up to six small values in session storage for the open tab, cleared when it closes: the cloud budgets for the email read and the next-step tip (dio.cloudCalls.v1, dio.tipCloudCalls.v1), whether you said yes to the cloud for sample threads (dio.cloudConsent.v1), whether App Check failed in this tab, so nothing is sent again (dio.appCheckBlocked.v1, dio.tipAppCheckBlocked.v1), and whether this tab already found no on-device model, so later pages do not wait for it again (dio.onDeviceMissing.v1). None of them holds email text.

Retention

  • Saved orders and senders stay on your device until you clear them, clear site data, or remove the extension.
  • We keep no server copy of anything you check. This site has no database.
  • Google keeps cloud requests according to its Gemini API terms, linked above.
  • Vercel keeps aggregated analytics according to its plan limits, then deletes them.

Your choices

Keep everything on your device by choosing Do not send, or by using the extension only. Clear saved orders by clearing this site's data in your browser settings. Block analytics with any content blocker; the checker still works.
